The apparent, in-sample gains run larger; only the cross-fitted gains, which hold whole slides out, are shown. rung1's apparent intercept is ~0 and rung2's apparent slope is ~1 by construction (that is what each fit targets), so those are not reported as findings. Recalibration cannot improve discrimination; the cross-fitted Brier is the expected loss after local recalibration, not a validated result.

At small G the flexible rung is high-variance under cross-fitting: absence of a rung3 gain is evidence of linear miscalibration only insofar as rung3 had power to detect shape distortion. Limitations

The optimism correction in section 3 covers the recalibration step ONLY -- it does not cover model or ensemble construction. If the ensemble weights (the champion) were selected on slides inside this cohort, rung0 is itself optimistically biased, and this harness cannot detect it: predval evaluates the predictions it is handed and has no view of how they were produced. Only a cohort the ensemble was never tuned on could expose that bias.
